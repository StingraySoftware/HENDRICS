New tutorial, :ref:`targeted-searches-tutorial`, on searching around a
previously measured spin solution. It works through an example — a pulsar seen
at 0.728 Hz at MJD 56682 and 0.711 Hz at MJD 61100, searched at MJD 50000 — and
explains how the trials are counted, including why the number of independent
trials of a folding search cannot be taken as the band width divided by
``1/T``: the statistic is smooth in frequency, so the effective trial count of
its maximum depends on the threshold it is evaluated at, and Monte Carlo
simulations put the true count 3, 5 and 10 times higher than the naive figure
for :math:`Z^2_1`, :math:`Z^2_2` and :math:`Z^2_4`. Also documents the three
things that can catch a user out: that the ephemeris must be fixed before the
data are examined, that ``HENaccelsearch`` needs a narrow ``--fmin``/``--fmax``
to reach its full sensitivity, and that ``HENzsearch --fast`` and
``HENaccelsearch`` refer their candidates to different epochs.
